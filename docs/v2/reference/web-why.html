WHY REMMYND

You don't have a memory problem. You have two gaps.

A reminder can fail before the moment, and after it. Most tools only look at the first. Here is what the research says, and what remmynd does about each.

GAP ONE

You forget at the moment.

Remembering to do something later has a name: prospective memory. Failures of it make up 50 to 80 percent of everyday memory failures.

It gets worse when you are busy. In one experiment, 8 percent of healthy young adults forgot an intended action. With their attention divided, 24 percent did.

WHAT REMMYND DOES

Remind in layers

Text reminders lifted clinic attendance from 54 to 67 percent across 21 studies. Several notifications raised attendance by 25 percent. A single one managed 6.

GAP TWO

You remember, and still don't do it.

Intentions turn into action about half the time. Wanting to attend is not attending.

What helps is a specific plan. When voters were asked what time they would vote, where they would be coming from and what they would do before, turnout rose 4.1 points among those reached. The plain reminder call did nothing.

WHAT REMMYND DOES

Follow through

At the moment, the reminder carries the action: one tap opens the link. Afterwards it asks once: done, or not yet? If you want, it nudges up to three times.

A VERY SMALL SECOND BRAIN

A notebook can be part of your memory, if you can trust it.

In 1998 the philosophers Andy Clark and David Chalmers argued that a notebook counts as part of a person's memory when three things hold. Those three things are our product rules.

Always there
Works offline, on every device, from any link.
Easy to reach
One line to put something in. One tap to save it.
Trusted
It never loses a reminder and never cries wolf.
